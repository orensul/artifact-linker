The Headlines dataset for Sarcasm Detection addresses the limitations of previous Twitter-based datasets by providing a cleaner, more reliable source of data. It is collected from two distinct news websites: TheOnion and HuffPost. TheOnion is known for its satirical content, and headlines from its 'News in Brief' and 'News in Photos' categories are used to represent sarcastic examples. In contrast, HuffPost provides real, non-sarcastic news headlines. This dataset offers several advantages over Twitter datasets, which often suffer from noisy labels and informal language. The professional nature of news headlines ensures a formal writing style, free from spelling errors and informal usage, thus reducing data sparsity and enhancing the applicability of pre-trained embeddings. Additionally, the clear distinction between sarcastic and non-sarcastic content in this dataset results in high-quality labels with minimal noise. Unlike tweets, which may require contextual information from previous messages, these news headlines are self-contained, allowing for a more straightforward analysis of sarcasm. This dataset is particularly useful for researchers and developers working on sarcasm detection models, providing a robust foundation for training and evaluation.
