The PAWS dataset, titled 'Paraphrase Adversaries from Word Scrambling,' is designed to enhance the understanding of paraphrase identification by focusing on the importance of structure, context, and word order. It contains over 108,000 human-labeled pairs and 656,000 noisily labeled pairs, derived from Wikipedia and Quora Question Pairs (QQP). The dataset is monolingual, featuring English text, and is categorized under text classification tasks such as semantic similarity classification and scoring. It is available in three configurations: labeled_final, labeled_swap, and unlabeled_final, each with distinct data splits for training, testing, and validation. The labeled_final set includes human-judged pairs generated through word swapping and back translation, while the labeled_swap set contains high-quality pairs without back translation. The unlabeled_final set offers noisily labeled pairs for auxiliary training. The dataset is formatted in TSV files with columns for unique ID, two sentences, and a label indicating paraphrase status. Intended for research in paraphrase identification, PAWS addresses the challenge of high lexical overlap without semantic similarity, providing a robust resource for developing models that can accurately distinguish between paraphrases and non-paraphrases.
