The Semeval-2017 dataset is designed for benchmarking keyphrase extraction and generation techniques from abstracts of English scientific articles. It was introduced by Isabelle Augenstein et al. in the paper 'SemEval 2017 Task 10: ScienceIE - Extracting Keyphrases and Relations from Scientific Publications'. The dataset comprises abstracts from 500 scientific papers sourced from ScienceDirect open access publications, covering domains such as Computer Science, Material Sciences, and Physics. Each paper includes keyphrases annotated by student volunteers and expert annotators, with expert annotations prevailing in cases of disagreement. The dataset is divided into training, development, and test splits, containing 350, 50, and 100 articles respectively. Keyphrases are categorized into extractive, which are present in the text, and abstractive, which are not. The dataset supports tasks like keyphrase extraction and generation, and provides token tags in the BIO tagging format for sequence labeling tasks. This structure facilitates researchers in evaluating models using contextual language models for keyphrase extraction. The dataset is accessible for programmatic download, making it a valuable resource for developing and testing keyphrase extraction methodologies in scientific literature.
