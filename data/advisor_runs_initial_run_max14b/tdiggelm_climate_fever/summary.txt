The ClimateFever dataset is a collection of 1,535 real-world claims related to climate change, sourced from the internet. Each claim is paired with five manually annotated evidence sentences retrieved from English Wikipedia, resulting in a total of 7,675 claim-evidence pairs. The dataset is designed to support tasks such as text classification, text retrieval, fact-checking, and semantic similarity scoring. It is particularly focused on challenging claims that involve multiple facets and disputed cases where both supporting and refuting evidence are present. The dataset is monolingual, with all text in English, and falls within the size category of 1,000 to 10,000 entries. The data is structured with fields including a unique claim identifier, the claim text, a claim label indicating whether the claim is supported, refuted, or lacks enough information, and a list of evidences. Each evidence entry includes an evidence identifier, a label, the source article title, the evidence sentence, entropy reflecting uncertainty, and individual votes. The dataset is available in a single test split, with a total size of approximately 2.4 MB. It is intended for use in developing and evaluating models for verifying climate-related claims.
